## Excerpts

- Amid data- and AI-driven innovation, financial MyData (금융 마이데이터) is a scheme for integrated management and use of an individual's financial information scattered across institutions. It was first introduced in January 2022 and relaunched as 2.0 in June 2025 with improvements in four areas: expanded information, sales activation, convenience, and information protection.

- Financial MyData adoption grew quickly among banks, card issuers, securities firms, and fintechs, but insurance industry participation stands at 3 companies (under 5% of all MyData providers), a low rate relative to the industry's size.

- The low participation appears to come from a mismatch between the current scheme and the characteristics of the insurance business: (1) the information financial MyData provides (asset, liability, and spending records) does not match the information insurance risk assessment needs (disease, accident, health), (2) low digital touchpoints with customers limit data utilization, and (3) system build and operating costs are high relative to those benefits.

- Financial MyData may offer limited utility in traditional protection-centered insurance lines, but by lawfully collecting and using external financial information with customer consent, insurers can expect to expand into pensions and asset management or use it as a supporting tool for existing sales channels.

- As the scheme changes and usage expands, consumer protection and data security must be upgraded in parallel, and insurers should build their own security inspection systems.

**1. Background**

- With the government recently setting data- and AI-driven innovation as a core growth engine,[^1] the financial sector introduced the financial MyData scheme in January 2022 and reorganized it into financial MyData 2.0 in June 2025.

- Financial MyData is a scheme under which an individual can demand transmission of their own credit information scattered across financial companies; the information is delivered to a MyData provider, which manages and uses it in an integrated way.

- Cumulative service subscriptions grew rapidly, from 14 million (1,400만) at launch (January 2022) to 165.31 million (1억 6,531만) in May 2025, an increase of about 11.8x.[^2]

- Financial MyData 1.0 was criticized for the limited scope and specificity of provided information, complex consent and usage procedures, and restricted sales practices. The Financial Services Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service overhauled the scheme around the four areas of expanded information, sales activation, convenience, and information protection, launching MyData 2.0 in June 2025.

**MyData 1.0 limitations and MyData 2.0 improvements by area**

| Area | MyData 1.0 limitation | MyData 2.0 improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expanded information | Bulk asset lookup restricted | Bulk asset and liability lookup allowed at the sector level |
| | Payment history at the PG (payment gateway) level | Detailed payment history at the final-seller (merchant name) level |
| | Public-data linkage restricted | Public-data linkage services expanded |
| Sales activation | Face-to-face, concurrent, and ancillary business restricted | Face-to-face sales and concurrent/ancillary business scope expanded |
| | Data transmission standards lacking | Periodic and non-periodic transmission scope specified (once a week to once a month) |
| Convenience | Closing small or inactive accounts and transferring balances required each financial company's app or Account Info\* | Immediate closure in the MyData app |
| | Asset lookup consent in 2 steps | Simplified to 1 step |
| | Subscription period 1 year (annual renewal) | Selectable 1~5 years |
| | Eligible from age 19 | Eligible from age 14 |
| Information protection | Weak security standards for third-party provision | Security strengthened for third-party provision via a secure provision system |
| | Data accumulation in long-inactive accounts | Automatic cleanup of user information after long inactivity |
| | Subscription management scattered | Integrated management of subscription records and third-party provision records introduced\*\* |

\* Account Info (어카운트인포): an integrated financial product inquiry and management service in the user's own name, operated and provided by the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (금융결제원)

\*\* From any MyData provider's app, users can view their full subscription records and third-party information provision records, and disconnect them if needed

- Unlike banking, cards, securities, and fintech, where financial MyData services have spread in asset management and spending analysis, insurance industry participation is limited.

- As of April 2022 there were 66 providers in total (56 full licenses, 10 preliminary licenses),[^3] and the insurance industry accounted for only 4 companies (3 full, 1 preliminary).

- Participation by sector as of April 2022: banks (10), financial investment (9), cards and capital (9), insurance (4), savings banks (2), mutual finance (1), CB (2), fintech and IT (30).

- After the MyData 2.0 overhaul, as of October 2025 the number of fully licensed providers decreased slightly to 62,[^4] the sector composition largely held, and insurance industry full-license participation also held at 3 companies.

- Participation by sector as of October 2025: banks (10), financial investment (10), cards and capital (10), insurance (3), savings banks (2), mutual finance (1), CB (2), fintech and IT (23), public institutions (1).

- The slight decrease in total providers versus 1.0 appears to come from some providers returning their licenses, citing weak profitability, business-model limits, and API (Application Programming Interface) call charges.[^5][^6]

- The insurance industry accounts for under about 5% of all financial MyData providers, a low participation rate relative to its asset share within the financial industry.

- Across the combined six major domestic financial sectors, the insurance industry holds a 20% asset share, second only to banks.[^7]

- This report reviews the state of financial MyData participation, analyzes the causes of the low rate, and proposes response directions for financial MyData utilization.

**2. Insurers' Use of Financial MyData and Causes of Low Participation**

- Insurers participating as financial MyData providers can link other financial companies' data to give integrated views of assets, liabilities, and spending, and are widening use of their insurance apps by connecting lookup results to coverage checkups, consultations, and processing workflows.

- Participating insurers provide policy lookup, coverage analysis, and discovery of dormant assets and unclaimed insurance benefits, and are pursuing service differentiation by applying MyData-sourced information to retirement planning, document simplification, and integrated asset and coverage checkups.

- (Company A) Lets customers view financial assets and pension assets together in its app via financial MyData, then feeds the results into retirement planning simulations that present expected pension income and preparedness, helping consumers judge their retirement readiness and leading into pension and protection product consulting.

- (Company B) Combines public MyData such as tax, pension, and national health insurance payment histories to replace administrative documents needed at the enrollment and claim stages with data transmission, reducing hassle for consumers and lowering the input and verification workload in internal underwriting and claims handling for operating efficiency.

- (Company C) Provides integrated lookup and analysis of external financial assets, coverage analysis of held policies, and goal-and-reward participation content that consumers set up themselves, driving app dwell time and revisits and prompting asset checkups and coverage top-up actions.

- However, the information financial MyData provides centers on assets, liabilities, and spending patterns; it does not include information on insurance's core risks such as disease incidence probability, medical utilization, and accident risk, which limits its usefulness.

- In 2024, protection-type insurance (disease, death, injury, etc.) accounted for about 49% of the life insurance industry's premium income, and long-term personal lines accounted for about 52% for the non-life insurance industry; more than half of insurance revenue requires a medical and health information base, not financial data.

- Medical and health data, however, is operated under a separate scheme (medical MyData) under the Ministry of Health and Welfare and is not provided through financial MyData.

- As a result, insurers find it difficult to do tailored coverage design or product recommendations with financial data alone, and difficult to connect to credit scoring, asset management services, and personalized product recommendations built on financial transaction data the way banks, securities firms, and card issuers do.

- Insurance also has lower transaction frequency than other financial products and is sold mainly face-to-face, so customer touchpoints on digital platforms are limited, and the perceived effect of MyData-based personalized services is small.

- Financial MyData services are delivered through the provider's own app, so how often customers visit the platform is a major determinant of service utilization.

- Because insurance has few major transaction events such as enrollment, renewal, and claims, customers have little incentive to visit an insurer's platform frequently to explore information.
