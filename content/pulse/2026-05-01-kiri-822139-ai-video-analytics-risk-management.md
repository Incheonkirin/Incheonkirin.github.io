**Published**: 2026-05 · **Category**: Issue Analysis · **Author**: Han Jin-hyun · **Source**: [Korea Insurance Research Institute](https://www.kiri.or.kr/report/reportList.do)

## Summary

Technology that analyzes CCTV and other video in real time with AI algorithms has recently drawn attention as a new risk management tool, but it raises concerns about privacy violations and misuse, and Korea and other major jurisdictions apply related regulations. Expanding the use of video AI analytics is needed to improve risk management efficiency and reduce the social cost of accidents, but since AI regulation is at an early stage, the impact of regulatory changes on the technology's use should be monitored continuously.

## Excerpts

- As AI spreads into a wide range of areas, AI-based video analysis is drawing attention as a new risk management approach.

- For example, systems now apply AI to workplace CCTV feeds to check compliance with personal protective equipment (PPE) rules and to detect speeding by container vehicles driven by workers.

- Orlen, Poland's state-owned energy company, introduced AI on workplace CCTV and reduced workers' risky behavior by 35% in the first two weeks and workplace accidents by 75% over 16 weeks.[^1]

- The Virginia Port Authority in the US analyzed recorded footage with AI and cut speeding by container transport vehicles in the port by 50% and safety vest violations by 15%.[^2]

- Video AI requires up-front capital investment, but it can recover the initial investment by reducing the time and resources needed for safety management and security work.

- Of 140 companies in North America and Europe that used video analytics systems, 85% said they recovered their investment within one year of adoption and achieved cost savings beyond it.[^3]

- The market for video AI analytics used in risk management is also expected to grow substantially.

- The market for AI video analytics that checks PPE compliance is projected to grow from USD 1.32 billion in 2024 to USD 5.12 billion by 2033, a compound annual growth rate of 16.7%.[^4]

- Korea is also working, in both the public and private sectors, to prevent accidents with AI video analytics and to minimize damage when accidents occur.

- Local governments analyze CCTV footage with AI to speed up accident response, such as detecting road flooding and searching for missing persons.

- Gyeonggi Province,[^5] Sejong City,[^6] and Busan[^7] are building systems that predict or detect flooding by analyzing CCTV footage with AI, and Seoul plans to build an AI video analysis system this year using about 123,000 CCTV cameras to quickly trace a missing person's route from the reported appearance and direction of movement.[^8]

- The private sector also uses AI video analytics to strengthen safety and security at industrial sites, and is working to make the data collected in the process usable for insurance services.

- An attempt is under way to link data collected through Company L's AI video analytics to insurers through a platform provided by Company B.[^9]

- AI video analytics can dramatically improve the efficiency of supervision, but concerns about privacy violations and misuse have led major countries to introduce and apply regulations.

- Unlike the conventional approach, which requires staff to watch the footage, AI video analytics enables supervision at all times with less manual oversight and can speed up incident recognition and response, so stronger supervision and lower labor costs can be expected.

- However, information collected on video could be used for performance evaluation beyond safety and security management, or leak through hacking, raising concerns about privacy violations and misuse, so related regulations apply in Europe and the United States.

- In Europe, video-based supervision must follow the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR),[^10] and when personally identifiable information collected on video is analyzed with AI, the AI Act requires managing the risks of AI use, including building a risk management system and maintaining cybersecurity.[^11]

- Failure to comply with the AI Act carries a fine of up to EUR 15 million or 3% of annual turnover, whichever is higher.

- In the US, privacy regulations for personal information collected on video are applied state by state,[^12] and regulatory bills to control the risks accompanying AI use are also being proposed at the state level.[^13]

- Korea likewise needs to expand the use of AI video analytics to make risk management more efficient and reduce social costs, so public-private cooperation should be strengthened, while the impact of AI regulation on the technology's use should also be checked continuously.

- Expanding the use of AI video analytics can enable preemptive responses to accidents, minimize damage, and reduce social costs, while also contributing to easing companies' insurance premium burden.

- As noted above, local governments already use AI algorithms on CCTV footage to prevent flooding and search for missing persons, which can be expected to reduce the time and fiscal spending required for damage recovery.

- With the amendment of the Serious Accidents Punishment Act (중대재해처벌법) strengthening executives' duty to ensure safety and health, workplace risk management using AI video analytics can be expected to mitigate executives' legal risk.

- If effective risk management reduces workplace accidents, companies' liability losses shrink and their insurance premium burden can also ease.

- Cooperation between public CCTV footage and disaster response systems on one side and private AI technology on the other can raise society-wide risk management capacity.

- The Ministry of the Interior and Safety has released, on a pilot basis, an AI model and training data for detecting flooding from CCTV footage, and is improving its performance based on feedback from the companies, research institutes, and citizens who use it.

- The private sector is also building systems that monitor wildfires, floods, and other hazards using AI video analytics and map infrastructure. If these systems train on CCTV footage released as public data by the central and local governments and connect with disaster response systems such as the Korea Forest Service and fire headquarters, society-wide risk management capacity can be expected to improve.

- However, since domestic AI regulation is at an early stage of adoption, its content may change, and the impact of future regulatory changes on video AI technology should be watched closely.

- Like Europe and the United States, Korea protects personal information collected on video under the Personal Information Protection Act, and the recently introduced AI Basic Act (인공지능법) brings regulation of AI technology used on video.

- That said, the AI Basic Act's criteria have been criticized as ambiguous, and since AI video analytics uses personally identifiable information, it may be affected by whether it is judged to fall under 'high-impact AI'. The impact of future regulatory changes on the technology's use should be monitored continuously.
