## Summary

For the insurance industry to innovate its services and expand into new areas, consumer evaluations and perceptions of digital insurance services, insurers' information collection practices, and personal data use will matter. This report introduces the results of the 2024 insurance consumer behavior survey and uses them to examine tasks for improving digital insurance services from the consumer's perspective.

First, digital insurance services had lower usage rates and usage frequency than services in other financial sectors, and the lowest service satisfaction. Second, regarding digital non-financial services, the survey found that negative attitudes toward the personalized digital health-management services insurers offer stem from the possibility that collected information could be used against the consumer or misused for purposes other than service provision, and from doubts about insurers' capacity to handle sensitive data. Third, trust in insurers as collectors, users, and managers of personal information was lower than trust in banks, and similar to trust in card companies and securities firms. In the personal information collection process, however, insurers, together with securities firms, received the lowest ratings among financial companies for easy and transparent explanations, ease of withdrawing consent, and ease of partial consent. Fourth, an online consumer experiment showed that providing information about insurers' use of health and medical data can positively affect consumer attitudes toward that use. When both concrete benefits and the strength of safeguards on data use and processing were presented, consumers were more likely to hold a positive attitude toward insurers' use of health and medical data.

Based on the survey results, the report proposes the following tasks for improving digital insurance services to raise consumer satisfaction and trust. First, expanding digital insurance services requires improving the services in every dimension: usefulness, convenience, design, and reliability. This calls for investigating concrete consumer usage experiences and needs, and for finding ways to raise the frequency of digital service use. Second, raising trust in insurers as collectors, users, and managers of personal information requires improvements in transparent explanations during requests for consent to collect and use personal information, ease of partial consent, and ease of withdrawing consent. Third, improving consumer perceptions of insurers' use of health and medical data requires raising trust in insurers as collectors, users, and managers of personal information, and deepening consumers' understanding of the concrete benefits they can receive and the strength of safeguards on data use and processing.

## Excerpts

- As digital financial services became widespread, non-face-to-face financial transactions increased, and financial transactions on mobile devices have grown recently

- The share of deposits, withdrawals, and fund transfers made through banks' internet and mobile banking rose from 60.4% at the end of 2019 to 84.6% at the end of 2024 (by transaction count)

- In insurance as well, the share of premium income from the CM channel (online and mobile) rose from 0.1% for life insurance (by initial premiums) and 4.5% for non-life insurance (by direct premiums) in 2019 to 0.4% and 19.6% respectively in 2024

- Digital financial services benefit consumers through speed and convenience and can raise social welfare through market competition and innovation, but they can also create new risks with negative effects on consumers

- New kinds of consumer problems are appearing: expanded cyber risk, indiscriminate collection or use of information, inappropriate use of or errors in AI and big-data analysis, new types of financial fraud, reckless lending and investment, and digital financial exclusion of older consumers (OECD 2018; OECD 2020; GPFI 2018)

- Korean financial authorities, to prevent and mitigate consumer problems arising from digital financial services, announced the Guidelines on Personal Information Protection in the Financial Sector (금융분야 개인정보보호 가이드라인) in 2016, and the Comprehensive Plan for Data Use and Information Protection in the Financial Sector (금융분야 데이터활용 및 정보보호 종합방안) and the Plan for Strengthening Personal Information Protection in the Financial Sector (금융분야 개인정보보호 내실화 방안) in 2018 (오병국 외 2024, Oh et al. 2024)

- Concerns about excessive information collection or use and about new types of digital financial fraud can create consumer anxiety and aversion toward digital financial services, and beyond that, distrust of the industry

- Last year it became known that GM, the US automaker, had accumulated driving-behavior data through its internet-connected service and shared it with insurers via the data-analytics firms LexisNexis and Verisk, causing public controversy (The NewYorkTimes(2024. 3. 11.), "Automakers Are Sharing Consumers' Driving Behavior With Insurance Companies")

- The issues were whether consumers were clearly informed about third-party data sharing when subscribing to the connected service, and whether the explanation was given in a way consumers could verify and understand

- The California privacy regulator investigated the case, and after class actions were filed over violations of privacy and consumer-protection law, GM stopped providing the data

- Therefore, for the insurance industry to innovate its services and expand into new areas, consumer evaluations and perceptions of digital insurance service usage, insurers' information collection practices, and personal data use will matter

- For an insurer to analyze data, it must obtain permission from the consumer (the data subject) to collect the information, or permission for another institution to share data with the insurer

- Evaluations of insurers' information collection practices and trust in insurers as personal information managers therefore matter for the data analysis and use behind service development, and will also affect consumers' decisions to use new services and their trust in the insurance industry

- This report examines how consumers evaluate insurers' digital services and information collection practices, using the results of the 2024 insurance consumer behavior survey conducted by the Korea Insurance Research Institute, and reviews tasks for improving digital insurance services (변혜원 외 2025, Byun et al. 2025)

- The survey covered current use of finance-related digital services, evaluations of services financial companies currently provide, willingness to use digital non-financial services offered by financial companies, perceptions of insurance product development using health and medical data, and experiences with consent to information collection and use

- The first survey was conducted online with 4,000 adults aged 19~69 (August 2024), followed by an additional survey of 1,600 respondents (January 2025)

- This chapter covers survey results on digital financial service use and satisfaction, willingness to use digital non-financial services offered by financial companies, evaluations of consent to and management of personal information collection and use, and perceptions of health and medical data use

- It first assesses current usage and consumer evaluations of the digital services insurers currently provide

- It then looks at willingness to use health-management services and MyData (마이데이터) services, the digital-platform fields among the non-traditional new business areas Korean insurers could consider

- It also surveys evaluations of insurers' personal information collection practices and trust in insurers as personal information managers, which will affect data usability, demand for new services, and trust in the industry

- Finally, it surveys consumer attitudes toward insurers' use of health and medical data for insurance product development, and checks whether explanations of insurers' data use or procedures can affect consumer attitudes

**1. Digital service use and satisfaction**

- Most respondents (95.8%) used digital financial services, and insurance online services and apps had lower usage rates and usage frequency than services in other financial sectors

- Among digital financial service users, the share using internet and mobile banking was highest (92.2%), and the shares using insurance and securities online services and apps were lowest (53.2% and 52.1% respectively)

- By frequency, the share of respondents who use the service at least once a week was highest for super apps and banking apps at 90.6% and 88.9%, while insurance apps were lowest at 19.3%
